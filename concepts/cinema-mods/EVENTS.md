# Stories for the lobby and auditorium

[Watch the event screenings](events.html). The gallery includes recordings with real chatters, including three auditorium preshows.

## Run an event

Open **Lobby** or **Auditorium**, choose a title under **Timed events**, then click **Run event**. This works with Auto off. The director waits up to 90 seconds for a cast, a clear route and the right phase. The lobby has nine events. Manual [PEPE vs. YEE](media/lineup/manual-pepe-vs-yee.mp4) and [Quickdraw](media/lineup/manual-quickdraw.mp4) can use two real active chatter identities when their emotes are missing. The auditorium offers Laser Choir, Aisle vendor and Popcorn run.

## Try an event in QA

Choose a title in the screening room, then **Try in the QA room**. The [dedicated QA server](http://127.0.0.1:18791/concepts/cinema-mods/events.html) and its real-chat relay can be started with [Start Looks QA](../vaporwave/qa/Start%20Looks%20QA.cmd). A named preview waits for the room, props and eligible participants. Warp Gate plays on an actual lobby-to-auditorium flight. Press **T** in its QA lobby to make the trip. The [timed lobby](http://127.0.0.1:18791/lobby-qa.html?everyone=1&events=1&relay=127.0.0.1:18792) and [timed auditorium](http://127.0.0.1:18791/auditorium-qa.html?everyone=1&events=1&relay=127.0.0.1:18792) choose their own events.

### Mirrorball Ballroom · 14 seconds

[Watch the revised dance](media/lineup/mirrorball-walk.mp4). The ball drops beside the jukebox and guests gather on the clear carpet underneath it. The front dancer takes a low side step and kicks. After a beat the flanking dancers repeat the move, jump together and hold a final pose. The ball rises and the guests walk back. Sidewalk guests use the front entrance. Reduced motion holds the camera steady and softens the choreography.

### PEPE WINS, Aristortle and Infinity Foyer

In [PEPE WINS](media/lineup/pepe-wins-cards.mp4), eight chatters draw their cards from behind their backs, pose with them overhead and put them away. [Aristortle](media/lineup/aristortle-street.mp4) follows the public lobby exit and steps into the path of a passing vehicle. The [pavement reflection](media/lineup/wet-pavement-reflection.mp4) follows the rendered emote through each hop and landing.

In [Infinity Foyer](media/lineup/infinity-foyer-chat-message.mp4), a guest who has passed the usher walks down the hall to Screen 3. Three smaller copies wave from behind the red double doors. The guest slams the doors, says “wtf” in their regular chat bubble, and walks on to their assigned screening. The control panel’s **Bubble look** applies to that line and to [remarks from guests passing the Dear Leader poster](media/lineup/dear-leader-chat-message.mp4). The 15-second performance starts after the guest reaches the doorway.

The Moody room uses the **Ticket stub** setting in these recordings. Look for the guest’s “wtf” at 0:24 in [Infinity Foyer](../../roxy3d/qa-night/native-bubbles-27nov/infinity-ticket.mp4) and the passerby’s line at 0:04 in the [Dear Leader clip](../../roxy3d/qa-night/native-bubbles-27nov/dear-leader-ticket.mp4).

### Black Hole Matinee

[Watch the event](media/lineup/black-hole-chat.mp4). Tickets slide into the opening ring. Two to four chatters react through the ordinary speech queue before spiraling into the ring and emerging at the claw machine. The panel’s selected chat bubble is used for each speaker.

## How the timing works

Black Hole Matinee and Mirrorball Ballroom run on the large-show timer. The smaller-show rotation draws from eligible lobby performances every 3–6 minutes. The current screening list is at the top of the gallery. The remaining notes record earlier concept screenings.

## Lobby incidents

| Treatment | Beats and payoff | Cast, camera, and build |
|---|---|---|
| **Thermal Receipt** · 14 s | 0–3: the ticket is already longer than the counter. 3–10: the next two people successively catch the paper and pass it over their heads. 10–14: the clerk tears it, folds it once, and solemnly hands over a strip still trailing across the floor. | Use an occupied ticket window and up to three people in that line. Wide or medium. Anchor a single ribbon to the printer and hand positions. If service completes early, let its free end fall. First candidate after the two leads. |
| **Mercury Bath** · 10 s | A bead rolls off the marquee into a puddle. A pedestrian pauses at the curb. Their reflection takes two extra steps, turns to check where they went, and hurries back into alignment. | One visible pedestrian passing a wet patch. Frame both feet and reflection. Draw one clipped, inverted copy of that person's emote. If they leave, the puddle ripples flat. |
| **Infinity Foyer** · 15 s | Screen 3 opens. The far, middle and near copies of the guest wave in turn. The guest slams the doors and says “wtf”. | Use the active lobby's door animation and clip the reflections to its opening. Ordinary door traffic takes priority. |
| **Scanline Elevator** · 9 s | A floor arrow lights under two people. A rectangle of carpet rises just high enough for them to peer over the concession counter. The clerk looks up. The down arrow lights and both descend, trying to look casual. | Pick adjacent waiting guests. Keep a fixed medium shot with the counter lip visible. Lift the displayed guests with the carpet while their shadows remain anchored. Use the lip to establish height. |
| **Lenticular Marquee** · 8 s | A printed star follows a passerby with its eyes. The passerby backs up and the eyes follow back. Someone approaches from the other direction. The eyes split the difference and stare straight ahead. | Trigger on a real poster crossing. A second pedestrian is an optional ending. Two or three poster-local frames suffice. Keep the normal camera so noticing it feels like finding a detail. |
| **Folding Screen** · 12 s | A loose standee opens one fold, then another around a visitor. Their emote appears above the panels in succession. They emerge from the far end just as someone folds the whole contraption flat. | One idle guest with open carpet beside a standee. Add three depth-sorted hinged faces. Hold wide enough to see both exits. Collapse immediately if a door-bound guest needs the space. |
| **Pin Matrix** · 9 s | A guest presses a face into a pin board. The impression holds after they step back. A second face pushes through from the far side and startles them. With one guest, their own impression slowly reverses instead. | Prop insert near the arcade, with one or two available visitors. Draw the pin field from their current emote silhouettes. Frame the board and the startled viewer together. Clear the image when they leave. |
| **Contact Printer** · 15 s | A booth curtain closes. On each of three soft exposure cues, feet under the curtain change pose. A strip drops showing the two actual emotes. One person exits normally. The other comes out still tilted in the last pose. | Two idle chatters, a booth insert and three miniature still compositions. Save the emote appearance at each exposure. Keep the strip large enough to read during a short close-up. Discard it when the shot ends. |
| **Tidal Glass** · 9 s | A discarded cup rises on the first ripple. A chatter glides past the open entrance on the second. The person holding the door follows them with their eyes. The water drains, leaving the cup neatly upright. | Pick a real sidewalk passerby and an optional doorway observer. Clip ripples to one wet pavement patch. Keep the door and cup in frame. Use a short visual offset so walking and traffic continue normally. |
| **Horizon Drive** · 13 s | A striped sun appears *below* the curb in a puddle. Two headlights grow into a tiny car on a reflected grid road. Onlookers track it across their shoes. It vanishes down the drain and the ordinary neon reflection returns. | One to three curbside chatters. Low, fixed framing with real shoes and pavement visible throughout. Use a puddle mask, headlights, and one car silhouette. The sunlight lives entirely inside the reflection. |
| **Poster Peel** · 10 s | A corner curls loose. A curious chatter looks behind it and finds a tiny audience peering back. Both recoil. The paper slaps shut a moment before the next pedestrian reaches the poster. | One passerby. The miniature audience can be silhouettes clipped from the present crowd, shown only inside the torn corner. Hold a medium shot on the observer and poster edge. A passing vehicle can end the shot early. |
| **Parallax Cutouts** · 9 s | A printed hero tips sideways, revealing a real chatter lined up perfectly behind it. Everyone nearby turns. The chatter tries the hero's pose. The standee rights itself exactly as the pose finally matches. | One idle chatter, one standee, optional witnesses. Compose from the normal camera angle so the reveal is a silhouette match. A foreground prop and one actor pose are sufficient. |
| **Pinball Optics** · 12 s | A chrome ball escapes the claw machine. It pings off two rope posts as nearby guests dodge in sequence. The third guest simply steps aside. The ball rolls meekly back through the prize flap. | Lay out one deliberate path using machine and stanchion anchors. Pick actual nearby guests for the dodges. Hold wide to make the path readable. Skip when the machine is already active. |
| **Glyph Cascade** · 11 s | A marquee letter slips. A chatter catches it and looks up just as two more come loose. Nearby people pass them back. The sign relights letter by letter. The final character hangs upside down before flipping over. | Use a short piece of the currently displayed sign text and a few nearby guests. Conceal and restore only those glyphs in the prop's presentation. Respect sign updates that arrive during the scene. |
| **Vector Rebuild** · 10 s | A rope goes slack and unravels into cyan outlines. The first guest waits at the gap. An usher runs a light along the posts, rebuilding the rope from one end. The whole line advances precisely one place. | An occupied ticket line and a nearby usher. Stage the outline at the actual barrier. Time the last beat to a natural service advance. If service is slow, let a single relieved lean finish the scene. |
| **Leaded Glass** · 8 s | A patch of colored window light catches a departing guest. Their silhouette stays in one pane after they walk away. They glance back and the shadow slides out to meet their heels. | Trigger on a crossing through the entrance-light patch. Render one clipped silhouette in the pane. The camera stays wide. A guest who keeps walking still gets a clear ending as the color fades. |

## Auditorium preshow

| Treatment | Beats and payoff | Cast, camera, and build |
|---|---|---|
| **Laser Choir** · 14 s | The projector finds one row with cyan. The row bobs in sequence. A pink beam cues the opposite side to answer. A real message during the final beat can earn a short encore. | Start from the existing laser mounts and aperture cutout. Choose seated guests in two occupied rows and ripple by seat order. Hold wide so call and response read in one shot. Cap the encore at one. |
| **Prism Cathedral** · 10 s | A glass prism swings into the beam and paints colored pools across a seat block. Guests lean toward them. As the prism swings out, the colors briefly converge on one bewildered front-row chatter. | Use the calibrated projector apex, seat coordinates, and a clear final recipient. Keep the light on the seat side of the screen boundary. One medium-wide composition can show cause and payoff. |
| **Ribbon Plotter** · 12 s | A thin trace on the preshow screen rises as the first row stands. A wave moves back through the seats and the trace settles flat. One late guest finally stands, putting a solitary spike at the end. | Choose two occupied runs and one late participant. Draw the trace into a temporary preshow layer. Preserve the current slide clock, then yield the projector immediately when the show dims. |

## Verification · 27 Nov 2026

### Checking how a guest gets to an event

Start the recording while the guest is still on their ordinary route. Follow the same guest through the approach, the performance and the first steps afterward. For Infinity Foyer, leave enough of the hall in frame to see the guest turn toward Screen 3. Compare the visible feet with the projected walker coordinates during the approach and departure. A smooth glide can still cross a wall. Check a manual interruption during the walk as well as a complete take.

In the Normal and Moody lobbies, 1,058 routes were checked against the two art manifests. The controller checks exercise 16 cast events and Infinity Foyer, including early arrivals, interrupted walks, and returning to a previous activity. Separate cases exercise the restroom pair and the Yee–Pepe fight. On the accelerated six-hour timer, the lobby ran 49 photo booths, 14 Horizon Drives, five dances and 16 black holes, including two with the laser opening. The auditorium ran 77 Laser Choir preshows.
